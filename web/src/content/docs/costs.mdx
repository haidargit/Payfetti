# Every cost, measured

Every figure here was measured on Arc, not estimated, and every one is
reproducible from the repo without a wallet or a cent of real money:

```bash
python3 scripts/measure-gas.py
```

## Per payee

| | Gas | Cost at 20 Gwei |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batch overhead, once | 39,096 | $0.00078 |
| `recordRun`, once | 94,719 | $0.00189 |
| Each payee | 46,459 | **$0.00093** |
| Each payee, with an on-chain note | 55,589 | $0.00111 |

The per-payee figure falls as the batch grows, because the fixed overhead
amortises.

## Against the incumbent rails

| Rail | Fee per payment |
| --- | --- |
| International wire | $10–15 |
| Card / PayPal | $0.30–0.50 |
| ACH | ~$0.25 |
| **Arc** | **$0.00093** |

The point is the floor: below roughly $2, the fee eats the payment, so on every
conventional rail the payment usually does not happen at all. That floor is not
a tax, it is a hiring filter. If paying someone $2 costs $12, you never make the
offer.

### Where those three figures come from

They are published list prices, checked **October 2026**. They are not
measurements of ours, not quotes anyone gave us, and nothing in the app refreshes
them. The comparison shown in the interface multiplies them by the number of
payees and nothing more.

**Card / PayPal, $0.30–0.50.** The two ends are two real published fixed
components: [Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing) charges 2.9% + 30¢ per
successful domestic card transaction, and
[PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees) charges 3.49% +
$0.49 on a standard commercial transaction.

**We count only the fixed half, which understates what cards cost.** At $1.50 a
head, Stripe's 2.9% adds another 4.4¢ on top of the 30¢, so the real figure is
nearer $0.34. The fixed component is the part that creates the floor, and the
floor is the argument, so the percentage is left out rather than claimed.

**ACH, ~$0.25.** A typical flat origination fee for an ACH credit, which varies
by bank and provider rather than having one published price. Percentage-priced
ACH exists too, and is cheaper on very small amounts: Stripe's is 0.8% capped at
$5, which on a $1.50 payment is about 1.2¢. Still more than a hundred times our
figure, but worth knowing the flat fee is not universal.

**International wire, $10–15.** Deliberately well below what US banks commonly
publish for an outgoing international transfer, which is $25–50, and it counts
only the sender's side. Recipients and intermediary banks often take their own
cut on top.

Two of the three are therefore generous to the incumbent. That is on purpose:
erring against ourselves costs nothing when the gap is three orders of
magnitude.

## Two Arc specifics that bite

**Gas price has a floor.** Arc's minimum base fee is 20 Gwei, and a transaction
below it is dropped with no error and no receipt. The app quotes at the live
price but never below the floor.

**USDC has two decimal views.** The ERC-20 interface is 6 decimals, the native
view is 18. Transfers and `recordRun`'s `total` are 6-decimal. Mixing them is a
factor of 10^12, so balances are compared in one space and never via
`balanceOf`, which truncates.

## The block ceiling

One run must fit in a 30M gas block, which is roughly 642 payees bare and 539
with notes. The app caps a run at 500 so the limit never depends on the mode,
and enforces it before submission rather than at send time.

## Verified on-chain

gas, $0.0477. 49 paid and proven, 1 skipped (a zero address, where
